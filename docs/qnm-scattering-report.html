Planar reflection and transmission from QNMs

The normalized modes supply the complex pole residues. The fitted regular contribution comes from direct scattering samples after subtracting those poles. This benchmark measures how well the combined model interpolates a declared frequency window.

Sfit(k₀) = Σm Res(S, k̃m) / (k₀ − k̃m) + Bfit(k₀)

For each matrix element, we subtract the known pole term at 22 real-frequency points and fit a degree-10 Chebyshev polynomial to the remainder. That remainder combines direct scattering and the smooth-in-window effects of poles left out of the sum. The fit represents their combined effect without identifying an individual mechanism for each term.

What was verified

  • All four channel residues agreed with complex contour integration within 1.8×10⁻¹³ cm⁻¹. This verifies the pole calculation separately from any real-axis fit.
  • The degree-10 combined model had a maximum complex amplitude error of 5.54×10⁻¹² for TE and 1.29×10⁻¹¹ for TM over the held-out direct-scattering grid. The maximum flux-balance defects were 2.25×10⁻¹² and 2.12×10⁻¹¹.
  • The reconstruction is calibrated to direct data from each slab. The errors measure interpolation on these windows. A new structure or frequency window needs its own verification.

Do the poles improve the fit?

On the wider 3.2–12.0 cm⁻¹ window, all three models use the same 22 direct real-axis training samples and degree-10 polynomial. The direct-only baseline interpolates the complete scattering matrix. The other models fit the remainder after subtracting one or two Fabry–Pérot poles. All use the same 129 held-out points. Pole discovery and normalization are additional work for the pole-assisted models.

Cauchy reconstruction of the regular term

We sample the pole-subtracted scattering matrix on a complex circle centred at 7.6 cm⁻¹ with radius 5.1 cm⁻¹. Cauchy quadrature gives the regular term on the real window directly from those contour values. Comparing all contour nodes with every other node gives a nested refinement check. Omitting the second pole makes the held-out amplitude check fail because the remainder still has a pole inside the circle.

In flux-normalized channels, reciprocity checks the difference of the two transmission entries. The unitarity defect is the Frobenius norm of S†S − I, including both off-diagonal terms. Passivity excess is the nonnegative part of the largest eigenvalue of S†S minus one. These are measured checks on the held-out grid. The model imposes no physical constraints during fitting.

Dispersive channels and absorption

The dispersive fixture uses β = 1.2 cm⁻¹, a 0.3 cm core with relative permittivity 4, vacuum on the left, and εr = 2.1 + 0.025 cm × k₀ on the right. A verified outgoing anchor at 5.25 cm⁻¹ fixes both exterior roots. The code continues those roots throughout the declared complex domain and checks them against the selected pole at 5.200981084 − 2.491817995i cm⁻¹.

The analytic errors compare left-incidence amplitudes with the independent slab formula at 41 real points. The physical defects use the held-out direct-scattering grid. Taking every other contour node changed the result by 1.61e−12. Reversing the selected exterior root, adding a threshold exclusion, and shrinking the declared region each produced a typed error. The real channels must remain lossless and propagating, and the caller declares the analyticity of the complex region.

A separate slab with core relative permittivity 4 + 0.12i and vacuum exteriors absorbed 10.94% of left-incident power at 5.25 cm⁻¹. Its held-out complex amplitude error was 4.20e−12. Its maximum full unitarity defect was 0.196 and its measured passivity excess was zero, consistent with loss without gain.

From poles to a local design model

The lossy multilayer cavity gives two reconciled poles in the declared finite search region. Increasing core thickness by 0.002 cm moved the poles from 4.710332865 − 2.046405806i and 9.382669866 − 2.413091013i to 4.682461685 − 2.033115319i and 9.329796821 − 2.393877916i cm⁻¹. The locally corrected poles agreed with an independent discovery at the new geometry. Local tracking carries conditional coverage until the next discovery audit.
